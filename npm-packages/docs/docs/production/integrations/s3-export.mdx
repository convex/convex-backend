<BetaAdmonition feature="S3 export" verb="is" />

S3 export continuously streams changes to your deployment's tables into an
Amazon S3 bucket that you own. Each table is written as an
[Apache Iceberg](https://iceberg.apache.org) table and registered in the
[AWS Glue Data Catalog](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/glue/latest/dg/catalog-and-crawler.html),
so you can query your data from Amazon Athena, Snowflake, Databricks, Spark,
DuckDB, or any other engine that reads Iceberg.

The first sync copies every document in the tables you choose. After that, the
export streams only changes: each insert, update, and delete is appended as a
new row within seconds, sent in small batches. There's no connector to run and
no sync schedule to manage.

Convex only appends to the exported tables. It doesn't update or remove existing
rows, so an exported table holds every version of each document rather than a
copy of your current data. To query your current data, select the most recent
version of each document, as described in
[Changes and deletes](#changes-and-deletes).

<ProFeatureUpsell feature="S3 export" verb="requires" />

## How it works

- **Your data stays in your account.** Convex only writes to the bucket you
  choose, under a separate path for each deployment. If you delete the export or
  your Convex project, the data stays in your bucket.
- **No long-lived keys.** Convex accesses your bucket through an IAM role in
  your AWS account. The role can only be assumed by Convex, using an External ID
  that's unique to your export, and you can delete it at any time to revoke
  access.
- **A change log, not a mirror.** Every insert, update, and delete adds a row
  holding the full document as of that change. Your queries pick the latest
  version of each document.
- **Typed columns.** Each top-level field in your documents gets its own Iceberg
  column, typed from your [schema](/database/schemas.mdx) or, if you don't have
  one, from the documents in the table. See [Table format](#table-format) for
  details.

## Setting up an S3 export

S3 export is configured per deployment, in three steps: choose a bucket, connect
your AWS account, and pick which tables to sync.

### 1. Choose a bucket

1. Open the
   [Integrations](https://dashboard.convex.dev/deployment/settings/integrations)
   tab in your deployment's settings in the Dashboard.
2. Click **+** on the **AWS S3** card.
3. Choose **Create a new bucket** or **Use an existing bucket**. New buckets are
   named `convex-export-<deployment-name>` by default, and the region defaults
   to your deployment's region.
4. Optionally, enter a **Prefix** to put the export in a folder within the
   bucket.
5. Click **Continue**. This creates the export and its External ID, but nothing
   syncs until you click **Save** in step 3.

<Screenshot
  story="docs/pages/project/deployment/settings/Integrations#Configure S3 Export"
  alt="The Bucket step of the S3 export setup"
/>

Convex writes the export to
`s3://<bucket>/<prefix>/<deployment-name>/data-lake/` (or
`s3://<bucket>/<deployment-name>/data-lake/` if you don't set a prefix). The
tables are registered in a Glue database named after your deployment, with
hyphens replaced by underscores. For example, `musical-otter-456` becomes
`musical_otter_456`.

:::note

The bucket, region, and prefix can't be changed once the export is created. To
change them, delete the export and create a new one, which copies all of your
tables again. The new export reuses the same Glue database and replaces the old
export's Glue tables, though the old export's files stay in S3.

:::

### 2. Connect AWS

Next, create the IAM role Convex uses to write to your bucket. You can do this
from the AWS console or with Terraform. Both create the same role, and the
Dashboard fills in every value for you.

<Screenshot
  story="docs/pages/project/deployment/settings/Integrations#Configure S3 Export Connect AWS"
  alt="The Connect AWS step, with an AWS account ID entered"
/>

**Using the AWS console**

1. Click **Open in AWS**. This opens a CloudFormation quick-create page with
   every parameter already filled in.
2. Check the IAM acknowledgement box and click **Create stack**. The stack
   usually takes a minute or two to create.
3. Back in the Dashboard, enter your 12-digit **AWS account ID** (you can also
   paste the stack's ARN). Your account ID is in the account menu in the top
   right of the AWS console.

**Using Terraform**

1. Open the **Terraform** tab and click **Copy Terraform**.
2. Add the snippet to your Terraform configuration and run `terraform apply`.
   Make sure your AWS provider is set to the export's region, since the snippet
   creates the bucket and grants Glue access in the provider's region.
3. Back in the Dashboard, enter your **AWS account ID**.

<Screenshot
  story="docs/pages/project/deployment/settings/Integrations#Configure S3 Export Terraform"
  alt="The Terraform tab of the Connect AWS step"
/>

The Dashboard shows the role Convex will connect to,
`arn:aws:iam::<account-id>:role/convex/ConvexIntegrations-<external-id>`. Click
**Next: sync settings** to continue. The role is saved when you click **Save**
in the next step.

### 3. Choose tables and save

By default, Convex exports every table, including tables that belong to
[components](/components/overview.mdx) like aggregates and workpools. Uncheck
**Include component tables** to export only your app's own tables, or open
**Choose specific tables** to pick individual tables. You can also choose
whether tables you add later are exported automatically.

<Screenshot
  story="docs/pages/project/deployment/settings/Integrations#Configure S3 Export Sync Settings"
  alt="The Sync settings step of the S3 export setup"
/>

Click **Save** to start the first sync. The integration card shows **Starting
first sync**, followed by the progress of the initial snapshot. Once the export
has caught up with your deployment, the card shows **Active** along with the
time of the last sync.

<Screenshot
  story="docs/pages/project/deployment/settings/Integrations#S3 Export Active"
  alt="An S3 export integration card that is active and up to date"
/>

You can change which tables are exported at any time. Tables you're already
exporting keep their progress, newly added tables sync from the beginning, and
removed tables stay in S3 but stop updating.

If you click **Finish later** before saving, the export stays pending. To pick
up where you left off, open the **⋮** menu on the card and click **Configure**,
or click **Discard export** to delete it. Each deployment can have one S3
export.

## Permissions

The role created by CloudFormation or Terraform can only write the export's
files and register its tables in Glue:

| Permission                                                                                    | Scope                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| `s3:GetBucketLocation`                                                                        | The bucket                                            |
| `s3:ListBucket`                                                                               | `<prefix>/<deployment-name>/data-lake/`               |
| `s3:GetObject`, `s3:PutObject`, `s3:DeleteObject`, `s3:AbortMultipartUpload`                  | Objects under `<prefix>/<deployment-name>/data-lake/` |
| `glue:GetDatabase`, `glue:CreateDatabase`                                                     | The catalog and the deployment's Glue database        |
| `glue:GetTable`, `glue:GetTables`, `glue:CreateTable`, `glue:UpdateTable`, `glue:DeleteTable` | Tables in the deployment's Glue database              |

The role's trust policy only allows Convex's export role to assume it, and only
with your export's External ID.

If the stack or Terraform creates the bucket for you, it blocks all public
access, encrypts objects with SSE-S3 (AES-256), and cleans up incomplete
multipart uploads after seven days.

  ignore_public_acls      = true

## Table format

### Table names

Each Convex table becomes an Iceberg table in your deployment's Glue database,
named like this:

```
<component>__<table>__<table-id>
```

- `<component>` is `app` for your app's own tables. For a component's tables,
  it's the component path with each `/` replaced by `__`.
- `<table>` is the table name in lowercase.
- `<table-id>` is a fixed-length ID that keeps names unique. For example, it
  keeps tables named `Users` and `users` apart, since Glue names are always
  lowercase.

For example, your app's `messages` table becomes something like
`app__messages__<table-id>`. To find the exact names, run `SHOW TABLES` in
Athena or browse the database in the Glue console. Convex won't touch other
tables you create in the same Glue database.

The `<table-id>` changes when a table is replaced, for example by
`npx convex import --replace` or by deleting and recreating the table. When that
happens, the export writes to a new Iceberg table with a new name and the old
one stops updating, so update any queries that refer to the table by name.

### Columns

Every table starts with these columns, followed by a column for each top-level
field in your documents, up to 1,024 field columns per table:

| Column            | Type          | Description                                                                                 |
| ----------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `_id`             | `string`      | The document's ID                                                                           |
| `_ts`             | `long`        | When this version of the document was committed, in nanoseconds since the Unix epoch        |
| `_convex_deleted` | `boolean`     | `true` if this row records that the document was deleted                                    |
| `_creationTime`   | `timestamptz` | The document's [`_creationTime`](/database/types.mdx#system-fields). Empty for deletes      |
| `_convex_rescued` | `string`      | Fields that don't fit their column, as a JSON object. See [Rescued fields](#rescued-fields) |

Document fields map to Iceberg types as follows:

| Convex type                      | Iceberg type             |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| `string`, `Id`                   | `string`                 |
| `float64`                        | `double`                 |
| `int64`                          | `long`                   |
| `boolean`                        | `boolean`                |
| `bytes`                          | `binary`                 |
| `array`, `object`, `record`      | `string` containing JSON |
| `any`                            | `string` containing JSON |
| A union of more than one type    | `string` containing JSON |
| A union of `int64` and `float64` | `double`                 |

Unions only count their non-null types, so `v.optional(v.string())` and
`v.union(v.string(), v.null())` both get a `string` column, as does a union of
string literals. A `null` value is stored as `NULL` in the field's column, or in
`_convex_rescued` if the field doesn't have a column yet.

JSON values use the same encoding as
[snapshot exports](/database/import-export/export.mdx): an `int64` is a JSON
number, and `bytes` is `{"$bytes": "<base64>"}`.

If a table has a [schema](/database/schemas.mdx) with validation turned on,
Convex creates its columns from the schema when the table first syncs.
Otherwise, it uses the types of the documents already in the table. New fields
get a column the first time a document containing them syncs.

Once a column is created, its type doesn't change. If you later change a field's
type in your schema, new values for that field are written to `_convex_rescued`.

### Rescued fields

The export never fails because of the shape of your data. If a value doesn't fit
its column, Convex writes it to `_convex_rescued` instead. This happens when:

- The value's type doesn't match the column, like a `string` in a `long` column.
  A `double` column does accept an `int64` it can store exactly.
- The field name isn't a valid column name, or differs from an existing column
  only by case.
- The field name is reserved by Iceberg or by the export.
- The table already has 1,024 field columns.

### Changes and deletes

Each exported table is an append-only log of changes. After the first sync,
Convex streams only changes: every insert, update, and delete adds a row with
the full document as of that change, so a single document can have many rows.
Deletes are recorded as a row with `_convex_deleted = true` and no field values.
Convex never updates or removes rows it has already written.

:::important

Querying an exported table directly returns every version of every document,
including deleted ones. To get your current data, select the most recent version
of each document yourself: keep the row with the highest `_ts` for each `_id`
and skip rows where `_convex_deleted` is `true`. Retried syncs can write the
same row twice, so treat `(_id, _ts)` as the unique key.

:::

For example, in Athena:

```sql
SELECT *
FROM (
  SELECT
    *,
    row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY _id ORDER BY _ts DESC) AS version
  FROM "musical_otter_456"."app__messages__<table-id>"
)
WHERE version = 1 AND NOT _convex_deleted;
```

If you query the current data often, consider saving this as a view or
materializing it into a table of your own on a schedule.

Convex keeps the last 10 Iceberg snapshots of each table, so time travel only
goes back a few syncs. To read changes incrementally, filter on `_ts` instead.
Convex doesn't compact the files, so tables that change often will accumulate
many small files.

## Monitoring and errors

The integration card on the Integrations tab shows the export's status:

- **Finish AWS setup**: The export doesn't have a role yet. Open it and finish
  [connecting AWS](#2-connect-aws).
- **Starting first sync**: Convex is connecting to your bucket.
- **Syncing**: The initial snapshot is running, or the export is catching up
  with your deployment. Open the card to see its progress.
- **Active**: The export is up to date. Open the card to see the bucket path,
  the Glue database, and when it last synced.
- **Failed**: Convex can't write to your bucket, or the last ten syncs failed.
  The card shows the reason.

Common reasons an export fails:

- The CloudFormation stack is still being created, or failed to create.
- The account ID is wrong, or the role was deleted.
- The bucket doesn't exist, or it's in a different region than the export.
- A policy in your account, such as a service control policy, blocks an S3 or
  Glue action.
- Something other than Convex modified the export's Iceberg tables, like an
  Athena `OPTIMIZE`. The export's tables should be treated as read-only.

Convex keeps retrying a failed export. Once you fix the problem, the next
successful sync marks the export **Active** again, with no need to save it
again. If the tables were modified outside Convex, delete the export and create
a new one.

:::caution

An export can only catch up on changes your deployment still has in its history,
which is about two weeks. If an export stays failed or pending for longer than
that, it can't resume, and you'll need to delete it and create a new one.

:::

## Deleting an S3 export

To stop the export, open the **⋮** menu on the integration card, click
**Delete**, and confirm. Convex stops writing to your bucket within a few
seconds. Your files and Glue tables stay in your account until you create a new
export for the deployment.

To fully remove Convex's access, delete the CloudFormation stack or remove the
role from your Terraform configuration. Deleting the stack keeps the bucket and
its files. Terraform can't delete a bucket that still contains files, so empty
it first or remove the bucket from your configuration.
